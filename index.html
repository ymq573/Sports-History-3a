THE DATA-TO-ROOM PLAYBOOK

What drives the room

Three bays contain a heat pump, humidifier, and fan for each team, alongside two fixed lights per team. Each uses the most recent completed regular-season game. Points scored and scoring margin (team points minus opponent points) become environmental setpoints. These are design translations, not measurements of actual weather or instructions for physical equipment.

Data sources: College Football Data API + U.S. federal holiday calendar (2024–2025), using the built-in verified civic calendar. Latest mode uses each team’s last completed game. Season replay cycles through completed weeks every 12 seconds; bye weeks carry the previous result. Change CFBD_SEASON on Render to replay a different season.

07 / RESEARCH THROUGH DESIGN

Artist Statement

My thesis work is concerned with stadiums and college sports. I chose to work with college football statistics because they offered a way to study the events that give these spaces meaning: scoring, competition, momentum, and the differences between teams. Rather than treating a game as a final score alone, I wanted to ask how its changing numbers might be experienced spatially.

In this exhibition, data from three college football teams informs the room's temperature, airflow, humidity, and light. Each team has its own heat pump, humidifier, and fan, plus two lights. Points scored can raise that team's heat-pump target or brighten its front light; scoring margins can shift its fan, humidifier, and back light. These connections are deliberate translations, not claims that a score has a natural temperature or that a win is inherently bright. They let me test how an abstract statistic becomes an atmosphere that a visitor can perceive.

This is an exploratory study for my thesis. By comparing what a sports statistic communicates on a screen with what it might communicate through an interior environment, I hope to learn more about how stadiums shape the experience of college sports, and how data might inform my own design work in those spaces.
